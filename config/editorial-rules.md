# AI Daily Digest Editorial Rules

## Goal

Cover meaningful AI changes broadly and reliably, with priority on:

- Consumer-facing AI products and capabilities.
- AI workflows for people who use AI in daily work.
- Practical automation, research, writing, coding, creative, email, calendar, browser, and knowledge-management changes.
- Pricing, availability, privacy, permission, copyright, policy, and risk changes that affect real usage.

This digest is no longer optimized only for a personal external-brain or agent project. Agent and workflow infrastructure still matter, but they should be weighed equally against consumer AI and practical tool changes.

## Include

Include an item when it changes at least one of these:

- What ordinary users can do with AI.
- Which AI tool or model a person or small team should choose.
- Cost, free tier, subscription tier, regional availability, or usage limits.
- A real workflow in writing, research, coding, design, video, audio, meetings, email, calendar, browser use, files, knowledge management, or automation.
- Platform direction from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, Meta, Apple, Amazon, NVIDIA, xAI, Mistral, DeepSeek, Qwen, Perplexity, GitHub, Cursor, Replit, Adobe, Canva, Notion, Zapier, or similar major tools.
- Permissions or risk: AI can send messages, write files, access customer data, operate production systems, make payments, change settings, or retain memory.
- A practical case study that a person or small team can adapt.

## Exclude

Exclude by default:

- Small bug fixes, SDK patches, maintenance releases, minor UI tweaks.
- Benchmark-only stories without practical product or workflow impact.
- Pure funding, valuation, stock, or acquisition rumors unless they change product access, ecosystem integration, or tool availability.
- Hype without concrete product, feature, code, documentation, deployment, or credible reporting.
- Generic marketing posts without new capability or real case detail.
- Narrow research papers that do not affect product usage, workflow, deployment, safety, or automation.
- Social-media-only claims that cannot be verified by official sources, reliable media, code, model cards, papers, or documentation.

## Practical Impact Score

Use this 0-10 scoring lens to select and rank candidates:

- Consumer availability: 0-2
- Workflow change: 0-2
- Source reliability: 0-2
- Personal/small-team actionability: 0-2
- Cost, permission, privacy, policy, or risk impact: 0-2

Recommended threshold:

- 7-10: include.
- 5-6: include only when the day is quiet or the item is unusually relevant.
- 0-4: exclude.

## Source Priority

When duplicate stories appear, prefer:

1. Official blog, docs, changelog, GitHub release, model card, or technical report.
2. Paper or benchmark report with clear practical significance.
3. Reliable media such as Reuters, Bloomberg, The Verge, TechCrunch, VentureBeat, MIT Technology Review, The Decoder.
4. Curated newsletters such as TLDR AI, The Rundown AI, Ben's Bites.
5. Community discussion only as a lead, not as the only source.

## Final Judgment

Every included item needs a practical judgment. Use concrete labels such as:

- 可以试用
- 值得关注
- 暂不急用
- 适合个人 workflow
- 适合小团队
- 适合企业
- 需要谨慎
- 先观察
- 不建议现在购买
- 适合作为长期趋势跟踪

Do not write generic claims like "this is important" without explaining why it changes usage, cost, risk, or tool choice.
